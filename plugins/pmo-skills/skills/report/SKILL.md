# Report

A report serves one reader and one of three purposes: to decide, to know what happened, or to see what is coming. It prioritizes, it covers the ground, and it adds the context the reader lacks. It is the reader's overview, not a summary of the sources.

## Before you write

- **Reader and purpose.** Who reads it, what they will decide or do with it, what they already know. Ask when unknown.
- **Sources.** Which exist, which you read, which are missing or stale. Name gaps as gaps.
- **Contradictions.** Where sources disagree, the reader sees it first.

## The thinking

**Prioritize.** What matters most to this reader comes first. Everything else supports it or is cut.

**Cover.** Read every relevant source. Link each fact to where it comes from. Say what could not be found.

**Add context.** What this connects to, what changed since last time, what it depends on, what a number is compared to. The reader should not need to have been there.

**Keep facts and judgement apart.** A fact carries a source and a date. Judgement is labelled as assessment, stays neutral, and names its reasons. A reader who rejects the assessment can still use the facts.

**Look for what is hidden.** Contradictions between sources, dependencies nobody stated, numbers that do not add up, a decision that was made without being called one. Name them.

**Run the audience test.** Name the reader. Write the questions they will ask. Check that each is answered in the text. Then go one step further: the follow-up each answer raises. Unanswered questions are listed at the end, never dropped.

## Methods to pick from

Pick what fits the reader and the purpose. None is required.

- SCQA: situation, complication, question, answer.
- Pyramid: answer first, then grouped support.
- MECE grouping for causes, options, or risks.
- Metric, trend, target, and the reason for the gap.
- Status colour with the reason for the colour.
- Options with one recommendation and the decisive reason.
- Timeline of what changed.
- Decision log: decision, date, owner, reason.

## Consistency

One term per thing across the whole report. Same units, same date format, same names. A number that appears twice is the same number.

## Deliver

Return the report, then open questions and follow-ups, then the sources. Apply write-human to the prose.
