# Write human

Same content, cleaner text. Create a direct, warm, sharp tone that matches the author and the purpose. Cut filler and jargon without flattening the voice.

## Before you rewrite

**Match the author's voice.** Read supplied samples first and copy their habits: sentence length, vocabulary, paragraph openings, punctuation, transitions. If they write short and say "stuff", do not hand back long sentences and "components". Without a sample, use direct, concrete prose suited to the reader.

**Make the minimum effective edit.** Fix errors, repetition, inflation, and passages that are hard to follow. Leave strong sentences alone. A rough draft with a real voice still sounds like the same person afterwards.

**Never invent.** Add no claims, numbers, sources, opinions, or examples the author did not supply. When a passage needs a fact you do not have, cut the claim or ask. Keep meaningful uncertainty and technical qualifications.

**Preserve human detail.** Specific observations, mixed feelings, unresolved tension, asides, self-corrections, first-person choices, varied rhythm. A single em dash, a formal word, or one "however" is fine; look for patterns across the piece, not single words.

## Core checks

**Say what happened.** Replace "a pivotal moment" with the actual change. Name the source behind "experts believe" or drop the attribution. Cut participle tails such as "highlighting the importance of" when they add nothing.

**Use simple verbs.** "Is", "has", "does" over "serves as", "features", "boasts". "Made a decision" becomes "decided". Keep a more specific verb when it carries meaning.

**One thing, one name.** Pick the clearest word for a thing and repeat it. Repeat the name instead of pointing back ("the task function", not "the former" or "D1"). Synonym cycling for variety reads as thesaurus abuse.

**Remove mannered prose.** Say what you mean rather than choosing a phrase for its sound. Cut staged reveals, hollow intensifiers, aphorism formulas ("X is the Y of Z"), and "This isn't X, it's Y" framing when the direct statement is clearer.

**Apply the portability test.** If a sentence could move unchanged to an unrelated piece, check whether it contributes a fact, mechanism, consequence, or necessary connection here. Cut it or replace it with supplied detail.

**Keep the reasoning connected.** Lead with the point, then the evidence. Keep subjects near verbs. Each paragraph builds on the last. Cut warm-up and restating conclusions.

**Match format to structure.** Lists for discrete parallel items and steps. Prose for connected reasoning. Tables for real comparisons. A paragraph hiding six options needs a list; a list chopping one argument into fragments needs prose.

**Control emphasis.** Emphasis comes from evidence and sentence order, not from bold on every point, decorative headings, or rhetorical questions. Sentence case for headings.

**Read for rhythm.** Vary sentence and paragraph length naturally. Keep contractions and fragments when they fit the author. Avoid both metronomic prose and runs of manufactured punchlines. No word-count or deletion quotas.

**Remove assistant residue.** Cut praise, acknowledgment loops ("You're asking about"), method narration ("Let me break this down"), reader-steering ("It's worth noting", "Here's the interesting part"), tool artifacts, and sign-offs. A document describes its subject, not the process that produced it.

**Write English as English.** When the author thinks in German, watch for carried-over constructions: noun chains ("the implementation of the migration of the data"), "in the framework of", "since 2020 I work", "already" and "also" placed as in German, verbs pushed to the end, "Sie" formality in an informal piece. Rewrite as an English speaker would say it. The same applies in reverse: German text keeps German rhythm and register, with no literal translations of English AI phrases ("eintauchen", "nahtlos", "zusammenfassend lässt sich sagen").

## Words that signal inflation

Replace in context; keep a term that carries precise meaning.

- Always: delve, tapestry, realm, paradigm, embark, beacon, testament to, robust, comprehensive, cutting-edge, leverage (verb), pivotal, seamless, game-changer, utilize, vibrant, thriving, deep dive, unpack, intricate, ever-evolving, holistic, actionable, impactful, learnings, best practices, synergy, in order to, due to the fact that.
- In clusters: harness, navigate, foster, elevate, unleash, streamline, empower, bolster, spearhead, resonate, facilitate, nuanced, crucial, multifaceted, ecosystem, myriad, plethora, transformative, cornerstone, paramount, overarching.
- Transitions to cut: moreover, furthermore, additionally, in today's, in conclusion, when it comes to, at the end of the day, that being said, let's explore.
- Structures to cut: vague attributions ("studies show"), false ranges ("from X to Y"), false concessions ("while X is impressive, Y remains"), generic conclusions ("the future looks bright"), fake-profound closing lines, emotional announcements ("what struck me most"), novelty claims ("a term nobody's naming"), "whether you're X or Y".

## Deliver

When writing your own text, apply these checks and return the text without a style report. When rewriting a supplied draft, return the text and a brief note of material changes. Re-read once for meaning, unsupported additions, surviving filler, and loss of voice. Stop when the requested edit is complete.
