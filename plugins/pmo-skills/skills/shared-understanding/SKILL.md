# Shared understanding

Pick the format that lets this reader hold this idea. One idea, one or two formats. Name the choice in one line and move on.

## Before you start

Who needs to understand it, and what they already know. The one thing they must take away. Ask when unknown.

## Formats

- **Decision memo**, for a reader who must choose, typically C-level. See below.
- **Before/after table**, for a change.
- **Reader's-eye walkthrough**, for a process change: what you will see, what you will do, what happens next.
- **Analogy**, for a new domain: a system the reader knows, mapped over, with the point where it breaks.
- **Stepwise reveal**, when the idea needs a build-up: one layer per step, each step complete on its own.
- **Term table**, when the words are the blocker: term, meaning, what it is not.
- **Strip the layers**, when abstraction is the blocker: remove one layer at a time until a plain sentence is left. Explain-like-I'm-five is the last layer.
- **Perspectives**, when the disagreement is about viewpoint: the same topic as three roles see it.
- **Quiz**, to check understanding after the fact: three questions, then what each wrong answer would reveal.
- **Handover**, for a reader with no outside knowledge: everything they need, nothing they cannot check.
- **Structure or flow**: a tree, a diff, Mermaid, or one HTML page. Use show-me when it is available.

## Decision memo for C-level

The executive reads the top, decides, and stops. Build for that.

1. **The ask** in one line: what decision, by when.
2. **Recommendation** in one line, with the single reason that carries it.
3. **Situation** in two lines: what changed and why it matters to the business now.
4. **Options**, two or three, including doing nothing. For each: cost, risk, time, and what it enables or blocks. Show the decisive difference, not a feature list.
5. **Cost of delay**: what happens if no decision is made by the date.
6. **Next step** once decided: who does what.

One page. Business terms: money, risk, customers, time. Numbers over adjectives. Technical detail goes in an appendix or a link. Keep the facts and the recommendation visibly apart, so the reader can disagree with the judgement without doubting the facts.

## Rules

Value the reader's attention. One idea per section. Short words where a short word exists. Repeat the term instead of pointing back at it. Say nothing about the format beyond the line that names it.

## Deliver

The idea in the chosen format. Apply write-human to the prose.
